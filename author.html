Why I wrote this text

I believe students learn math by doing math. I've been teaching discrete mathematics course since 1999. From the beginning, I recognized that discrete mathematics was a perfect setting to teach mathematical inquiry -- building examples and counterexamples, making conjectures, and justifying conjectures through proof. I wanted students to be ready for advanced courses in mathematics and computer science and for undergraduate research. But most of all, I wanted them to see themselves as mathematicians. People who do math and who enjoy doing math, at least when things work. And, I wanted to foster mathematical curiousity.

At first I taught from respected published texts (Rosen, Epp) but they were filled with answers. I wanted questions so that students could find the answers. What started as handouts for weekly activities (puzzles) and then daily activities (worksheets) evolved over the years to include inquiry-based exercise sets. Eventually, I had a set of materials that wasn't exactly a text but was good enough to teach from.

But as other faculty at Augsburg and faculty at other schools began to use the materials, I realized we needed an actual text. Better narrative. More examples. And an order that made sense. We began using the new edition in fall 2024, in our calculus-based MAT 302 Discrete Mathematical Structures (mainly for mathematics and computer science majors) and in our algebra-based MAT 202 Discrete Mathematics for Computing (mainly for management information systems majors).

With the encouragement and support of many folks in the PreTeXt community, and with countless hours of work by partner Matthew Richey, in spring/summer 2026 we made the leap to an online text.
